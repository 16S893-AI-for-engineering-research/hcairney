# Review: task-40_pretrained_actor_sweep

## 1. What was tested
- **Settings changed:** `exploration.sigma` (0.001, 0.003, 0.01), `learning.critic_updates_per_evaluation` (4, 8, 16), `replay.batch_size` (64, 128, 256), `replay.cell_sampling` (cells, volume), replay size and warm-up as a pair (32/8, 64/16, 128/32), and the network type as a set (relu or tanh).
- **Size:** 972 runs in 324 seed groups of 3 seeds each.
- **Families:** relu_cell, relu_volume, tanh_cell, tanh_volume.
- **Linked settings:** two sets of settings always changed together, so this sweep cannot separate their effects:
  - `actor.activation`, `actor.initial_weights` and `critic.activation`
  - `replay.capacity_evaluations` and `replay.warmup_evaluations`
- **Score:** the best check of `E_volume` during each run. Lower is better.

Main effects of each setting:
- **Critic updates per step (u):** the top 15 groups all use u=16. In every family the median gets better from u4 to u8 to u16. For example, relu_volume goes 9.61e-05, 4.53e-05, 2.02e-05, and tanh_cell goes 8.57e-04, 3.44e-04, 1.37e-04. u=16 is the largest value tried.
- **Network type:** the median relu group scored 4.75e-05 and the median tanh group 3.55e-04, about 7.5 times worse. 18 relu groups reach a mean below 1e-05; no tanh group does. The best tanh group is rank 39.
- **Noise (sigma):** in both relu families the middle value, 0.003, works best (u16 medians 5.72e-06 and 7.88e-06). The tanh families do best at 0.01, the top of the tested range.
- **Batch size, replay size/warm-up, cells vs volume:** small, mixed differences. Ranks 1–3 cover both sampling modes and two replay sizes.

## 3. Health concerns
- No group is flagged. There were no rejected steps, failed solves or failed checks. The highest solver effort per solve is about 261, well under the 450 limit.
- **The policy often gets worse late in training.** For the top group, the last check was 85.8 and 127.5 times worse than the best check on seeds 1 and 2. Rank 4 has one seed at 389.9 times worse. Across all runs, the median ratio is 9.3. So the score shows the best check, not a stable final policy.
- Some top-15 groups have a large spread, with std about as big as the mean or close to it: ranks 3, 6, 10, 11 and 13. Rank 3 in particular runs from 1.59e-06 to 7.38e-06.
- The best tanh groups have std about equal to their mean, so seed-to-seed results vary a lot.

## 4. Verdict
Rule used: a difference counts as clear only if the gap between means is larger than the std of both groups.

**Clearly better**
- The rank 1 group beats the same setup with u8: gap 2.80e-05, larger than both stds (6.85e-07 and 1.09e-05).
- The rank 1 group beats the best group below u16 (rank 16): gap 6.1e-06, larger than both stds (6.85e-07 and 4.00e-06).
- Sigma 0.003 beats 0.01 for the rank 1 setup: gap 2.45e-05, larger than both stds (up to 2.18e-05).
- Sigma 0.003 beats 0.001 for the rank 1 setup, but only just: gap 2.20e-05 against stds up to 2.16e-05.

**Unclear**
- Rank 1 vs ranks 2 and 3: gaps of about 5e-07 are within the spread. The top three are effectively tied.
- Volume vs cells sampling for the rank 1 setup: the gap of 2.29e-06 is smaller than the cells group's std of 1.75e-06.
- Relu vs tanh: the pooled pattern is strong (7.5 times worse median, 0 tanh groups below 1e-05). But in single matched comparisons, the large tanh spreads (e.g. std 4.6e-04) mean the gap does not pass the rule. The effect also cannot be separated from the pretrained starting weights, because they always change with the network type.
- Batch size and replay size/warm-up: no clear effect.

## 5. Proposed next setups
1. **More critic updates (u=32) on the rank 1 group.** Results improve steadily from u4 to u8 to u16 in every family, and 16 is the edge of the tested range. I expect the mean to fall to about 2e-06 with the same health. A result no better than u16 would mean the gain has levelled off.
2. **Lower the actor learning rate (1e-4 to 5e-5) on the rank 1 group.** Two of its three seeds ended 86 and 128 times worse than their best check, which suggests the policy becomes unstable late in training. A smaller step size should make the final policy closer to the best one. I expect a similar best score and much smaller late drops. This setting was not varied in the sweep, so confidence is moderate.
3. **Larger replay size and warm-up (256/64) on the rank 1 group.** The pooled median improves from 32/8 to 64/16 to 128/32, and the rank 1 group sits at the edge (128/32). This is the weakest of the three ideas, because the top groups do not separate clearly on this setting.
